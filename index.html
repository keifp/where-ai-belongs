Where AI Belongs

Every seminary course forms something AI could shortcut: close reading, proclamation, pastoral presence, honest reflection. Sort the ways students might use AI in your course, and this page drafts a syllabus statement you can adapt. Then test your statement against situations that come up in real classrooms.

Why this activity

  • It starts from formation, not fear. Faculty decide what each assignment is forming, then place AI around that. The statement explains the reasoning, which students follow more readily than bare rules.
  • It continues the conversation Candler has started. The Office of Digital Learning ran faculty workshops on generative AI in 2024 and 2025. This gives faculty something concrete to leave with: a statement for their own syllabus.
  • It works across programs. The same sort works for a residential exegesis course, a hybrid preaching course, or a D.Min. cohort, and the scenarios include Contextual Education and D.Min. research.
  • Accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA): every drag has a keyboard equivalent, card placement is announced in text, and color is never the only signal.
